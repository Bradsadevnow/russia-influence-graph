# Source pass 1 — Media nodes (transmission topology)

*Verbatim research log. This is the initial framing pass establishing that the
object of study is a **relationship graph** — who is demonstrably connected to
Russian state actors, by what mechanism, and how far the material travels — not
a list of "American media that says pro-Russia things."*

---

The deep-research job is framed correctly, and the initial source pass is already
surfacing the important distinction: this is **not** a list of "American media
that says pro-Russia things." It's a **relationship graph** — who is demonstrably
connected to Russian state actors, by what mechanism, and how far the material
travels.

The strongest anchors so far:

## 1. Tenet Media — the clearest covert-financing node

DOJ alleges RT employees secretly funneled **~$9.7 million** into a Tennessee-based
media company, representing roughly **90% of its bank deposits**, through shell
companies in Turkey, the UAE, and Mauritius. RT personnel allegedly exercised
editorial influence while concealing the relationship. [1]

Propagation chain:

**Russian government → RT → RT employees → shell companies → Tenet Media → six American commentators → YouTube/X/Instagram/TikTok audiences**

Tenet produced nearly **2,000 videos** and generated **16+ million YouTube views
alone** according to DOJ. [1]

Crucially, the commentators themselves were **not charged**, and several said they
didn't know about the Russian funding. So we distinguish:

> **Russian-controlled/funded node:** Tenet
> **Downstream personalities:** potentially unwitting distribution nodes

That's a *much* more interesting network than simply labeling everyone involved a
Russian asset.

## 2. Ben Swann / Truth in Media — a different kind of node

Not the same architecture as Tenet.

Swann's company **Rebel Media Productions** filed under FARA and disclosed
receiving **millions of dollars from Russian state media-related entities** to
produce RT programming. AP found the Russian-state-media relationship exceeded
**$6 million**, while other reporting documents individual payments and production
contracts. [2]

Then:

**RT / TV Novosti → Rebel Media Productions → Ben Swann → RT programming**

while simultaneously:

**Ben Swann → Truth in Media → U.S. audience → "Zelenskyy Unmasked" → American influencers**

AP found overlap between the two ecosystems: guests appearing in Swann's
U.S.-facing material also appeared on his RT programming, with overlapping
themes. [3] That's potentially a **bridge node** between an overt Russian-funded
media operation and ostensibly independent American political media.

## 3. The interesting question is the second and third hops

AP found that **"Zelenskyy Unmasked"** was promoted by American right-wing
influencers, including Donald Trump Jr., who apparently did not know about Swann's
Russian-media financial relationship. [3]

So we do NOT draw:

> Russia → Trump Jr.  ✗ (not supported)

The defensible graph is:

> **Russian state media → Swann's production infrastructure → U.S.-facing content → American influencer amplification → much larger American audience**

That distinction is a central methodological rule of the report.

## DOJ has basically handed us a map

Newer DOJ material describes RT as maintaining **"covert projects"** after RT lost
conventional Western distribution. It explicitly identifies the Tenet operation as
one of those projects and describes RT personnel managing covert distribution
channels. [4]

Broader architecture:

```
Russian state
  ↓
RT / TV Novosti
  ↓
covert funding + intermediaries + shell companies
  ↓
American media companies / producers
  ↓
American commentators
  ↓
platform distribution
  ↓
secondary amplification
  ↓
audience / political discourse
```

And **not every node knows every upstream node exists.** A state actor can inject
material into a network at one point and let ordinary media incentives do the rest.

## Three classes of American nodes

| Class | Example | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Direct state-connected** | Tenet, Rebel Media/Swann | Funding, direction, production |
| 🟠 **Bridge/amplifier** | U.S. influencers promoting Russian-derived material | Distribution/amplification, often without documented coordination |
| 🟡 **Downstream ecosystem** | Other outlets/personas repeating the narrative | Content propagation; no demonstrated Russian relationship |

Then we describe **propagation depth**:

- `Russia → intermediary → U.S. outlet → influencer → influencer → audience`
- vs `Russia → RT → American producer → American audience`
- vs `Russia → RT → content → American journalist/commentator → audience`

Those are *very different mechanisms of influence*.

**We're past "who is secretly Russian?" and into "what is the actual transmission
topology?"** — the much more defensible and interesting question.
